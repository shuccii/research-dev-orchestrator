# NAIST criteria for the master's thesis defense

Checked on 2026-10-16 from the public page [NAIST degree criteria](https://www.naist.jp/campuslife/degree/criteria.html). Re-check the page and the current program handbook before each use; this file is a summary, not the authority.

## Current public criteria (all programs)

The master's thesis is assessed as a scholarly paper in the field, with emphasis on novelty and usefulness, against six items (original Japanese, then a working translation):

| ID | Original | Working translation | What to prepare |
|---|---|---|---|
| N1 | 研究の背景や目的が十分に説明されている | Background and objectives are sufficiently explained | General problem → usual approaches → what is specific here → objective whose verb the results can satisfy |
| N2 | 研究の進め方や研究方法についての吟味が十分になされている | The approach and methods were sufficiently examined | Why this method, data and conditions; the alternatives considered; arbitrary thresholds justified |
| N3 | 実験データや理論計算の結果、調査結果についての整理と解析が十分になされている | Experimental, computational or survey results are well organized and analyzed | Each figure traceable to data/conditions; uncertainty, repeats or error bars; fair baselines |
| N4 | 得られた結果に基づく結論や新しい仮説への展開が論理的に十分に示されている | Conclusions or new hypotheses follow logically from the results | Objective → result → conclusion map; no conclusion stronger than its evidence; origin of any new hypothesis |
| N5 | 論文が学術的な記述法で適切に記述されている | The thesis is written in appropriate academic form | Structure, terminology, citation correctness and coverage, figure and table quality |
| N6 | 研究倫理の問題に適切に対応している | Research-ethics issues are handled appropriately | Citation and reuse, data handling and retention, authorship and acknowledgments, consent/permissions where relevant |

How it is decided (as stated on the page): each committee member evaluates every item; the student passes when each member's evaluation meets the set standard; results go to the faculty meeting after the thesis presentation (thesis review and examination). The page gives **no points or numeric thresholds**. The page also says committee members check the student's capstone record; any internal rubric in that system is not public and remains unconfirmed.

Doctoral theses use the same six items, with the prerequisite that the main part is published or to be published in peer-reviewed venues, and a public hearing (preliminary review) precedes the final examination.

## Historical field-specific document (do not treat as current)

The Materials Science area published a criteria PDF ([busitu_sinsa_kijun.pdf](https://www.naist.jp/publish/files/busitu_sinsa_kijun.pdf)) whose metadata dates to 2016 and whose title indicates a 2014/H27 update. It lists seven items, adds knowledge/literature and oral presentation, and describes separate 100-point scores for the thesis and for the presentation and Q&A, with 60 points required for each. Whether this applies to a current student is unconfirmed. Use it only as a reminder that the talk and the Q&A can be judged separately from the written thesis, so preparing the Q&A deserves the same weight as the thesis.

## NAIST-specific preparation notes

- Committees include members outside the student's subfield. Prepare non-specialist explanations of the background, the key method and the main figure.
- Confirm with the program office or advisor: presentation and Q&A lengths, slide and abstract templates, submission deadlines, language, and whether a lab-level rehearsal or advisor sign-off is required. Record each as confirmed (with source) or unconfirmed.
- Scores, pass predictions or "the committee will ask X" are never stated as facts.
