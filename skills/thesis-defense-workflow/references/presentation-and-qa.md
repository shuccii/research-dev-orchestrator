# Presentation and Q&A guide (science and engineering)

General guidance for thesis presentations. Institution or laboratory rules override it.

## Slides

| Check | Done when |
|---|---|
| Time budget | Slide count at most about one per minute; measured rehearsal time within the limit with margin |
| One message per slide | The slide title states the message; one slide does not carry two topics |
| Flow | Each slide answers a question raised by the previous one; the outline matches background → problem → approach → results → conclusion |
| Figures | Axes, units, symbols and data conditions are explained aloud; the takeaway is written on the slide; text is readable from the back of the room |
| Claims | Every quantitative claim maps to a thesis page/figure; wording matches evidence strength |
| Words and slides agree | What is on the slide is spoken; what is not spoken is removed |
| Script | A speaking rate the audience can follow (for example about 120–150 English words per minute), set by timed rehearsal rather than word counts |
| Conclusion | States findings and their scope, not a list of activities; limitations and future work are distinct |
| Backup | Backup slides exist for predictable deep questions (method details, extra results, failure cases, parameter choices) |

## Question categories

| Category | Typical question | Prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamentals | Explain [key term or principle] in simple words | Definitions in your own words; one equation or picture |
| Choices | Why this method/data/condition and not [simpler alternative]? | The comparison or reasoning actually done |
| Validity | How do you know the result is not caused by [confound, leakage, artifact]? | The check performed, or an honest limitation |
| Limits | Where does this not hold? | Conditions, range and boundary cases |
| Contribution | What is new compared with [closest prior work]? | One-sentence difference with a citation |
| Non-specialist | Why does this matter outside your field? | A plain-language purpose and impact |
| Ethics and data | Where are the data and code; can someone reproduce this? | Storage location, versioning, permissions |
| Future work | What is the next experiment and what would change your conclusion? | A concrete next step and its possible outcomes |

## Answering

- Listen to the whole question; restate it briefly if unclear.
- Answer in about 30 seconds: conclusion → evidence (slide/page) → limitation → next step.
- If you do not know, say so, state what you do know, and how you would find out. Do not guess numbers.
- Treat critical comments as information; thank, answer the substance, and note it for revision.
- Keep a list of the questions that were hard in rehearsal and rehearse those again.
