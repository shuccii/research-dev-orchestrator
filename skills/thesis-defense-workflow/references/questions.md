# Track Q: question bank and answering

Examiners test whether the student understands the phenomenon and the method, and knows the limits of the argument. Prepare for that, not for tricks.

## Building the question bank

Generate questions from the actual thesis, slides, the issue log and the known weak points. Add the committee members' public research areas (official profiles, publication lists) to anticipate questions from their viewpoint; use only public information and never present a guess about a person as fact.

| Category | Typical question | Prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Method principles | Explain how [method/instrument] works and why it can show [claim] | A one-slide principle explanation for each key method; avoid black-box use of kits or libraries |
| Fundamentals | Explain [key term or principle] simply | Definitions in your own words |
| Choices | Why this method/data/condition and not [simpler alternative]? | The comparison or reasoning actually done |
| Validity | Could [confound, leakage, artifact, missing control] explain this? | The check performed, or an honest limitation |
| Limits | Where does this not hold? | Conditions, range, boundary cases |
| Contribution | What is new compared with [closest prior work]? | One-sentence difference with a citation |
| Outlook | What does this mean for the field or other fields? | A broad but supported outlook |
| Non-specialist | Why does it matter outside your field? | Plain-language purpose and impact |
| Ethics and data | Where are the data and code; can it be reproduced? | Storage, versioning, permissions |

For each question record: why it may be asked, the evidence and slide to use, a 30-second answer outline (conclusion → evidence → limitation → next step), the likely follow-up, and the boundary of what can be claimed. If the defense or questions may be in English, prepare and rehearse the key answers in English as well.

## Answer patterns

| Situation | Do |
|---|---|
| Yes/no question | Answer yes, no, or both first, then one reason |
| Open question | Conclusion first, then evidence with a slide number |
| Unclear question | Restate it: "Do you mean ...?" and confirm before answering |
| The question rests on a wrong premise | Calmly state the premise used in the work and the evidence for it |
| You do not know | Say so, state what you do know, and how you would find out; do not improvise results or mechanisms |
| Criticism of a limitation | Acknowledge the limit as a fact, explain how the work handled or bounded it, and what would resolve it; neither defensive nor apologetic |
| Advice or a comment | Thank, then give your own view or how you will use it |
| A long one-to-one debate | Offer to continue after the session so others can ask |

Silence is worse than a structured partial answer, but a fabricated answer is worse than "I have not examined that."

## Rehearsal

Rehearse one question at a time with research-review-workflow in `defense` mode, answering aloud and timing answers. Record which answers failed and why, add backup slides or fixes to the issue log, and repeat the failed questions in the next session.
